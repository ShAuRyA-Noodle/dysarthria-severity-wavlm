Validity, not accuracy

A single correlation
is not validation.

Training-free metrics built on frozen self-supervised speech models are used to score dysarthria severity with no labels and no fine-tuning. They are almost always judged by one number — a correlation on one corpus. VERITAS-Bench audits any such metric against six confound controls and returns a PASS / WARN / FAIL report card. Every metric we tested passes its headline number — and fails at least one validity control.

The problem

The number says the metric works. The confounds say what it measures.

A metric can correlate with severity because it captures the disorder — or because it captures how long the speaker pauses, how loudly they speak, or simply who they are. On the trajectory-distance metric (ATD), the single reported ρ = 0.77 hides all three.

The instrument

Six controls. One verdict.

Feed VERITAS-Bench any per-speaker metric and a corpus. It runs six independent controls against documented thresholds and emits a machine-readable report card. A metric doesn’t win by scoring highest — it wins by surviving the controls.

01

Predictive validity

Within-corpus, leave-one-speaker-out Spearman ρ against clinical severity. The floor: does it recover severity at all?

PASS ρ ≥ 0.50, p < 0.05
02

Speaker-identity leakage

Naive reference vs a leave-one-control-out (LOCO) reference. A reference containing the test speaker’s own data inflates the score.

PASS Δ = ρnaive − ρLOCO ≤ 0.05
03

Silence confound

Does the severity signal survive silence removal? Retention of ρ on speech-only frames, plus the silence-frame fraction.

PASS retain ≥ 70% & silence < 50%
04

Energy confound

Partial Spearman controlling for mean speech energy. If loudness explains the correlation, the metric is an energy artifact.

PASS partial retains ≥ 70%
05

Cross-corpus transfer

Train a probe on the other corpora, test held-out. Single-corpus success that doesn’t transfer is overfitting to corpus idiosyncrasy.

PASS ρ ≥ 0.50, p < 0.05
06

Representation identifiability

Speaker-classification AUC of the features the metric uses. High identifiability is a leakage risk for any reference-based metric.

PASS AUC ≤ 0.70 (chance 0.5)

The battery discriminates. The trajectory-distance metric fails on silence (24% retained) and identity; the pooled probe is silence-robust (100%) yet still fails transfer and identity. A benchmark that returned FAIL for everything would be a hit-piece — this one certifies good design where the design is good. That is the point: it separates a metric that measures articulation from one that measures pausing, loudness, or identity.

Auditing the state of the art

We ran the strongest published metric through the instrument.

The phonological-subspace d′ metric (Muller et al., 2026) recovers severity across 3,374 speakers, 12 languages and five aetiologies. We reimplemented it from the published description and audited it — not to attack it, but to test whether the instrument is fair.

What holds up

The design is genuinely good: silence-free by construction, energy- and leakage-robust. The instrument certifies it — proof it discriminates rather than failing everything.

What it never checked
Speaker-identity AUCFAIL · .999

Their paper reports controlling token count and alignment quality — but not identity leakage or energy. VERITAS-Bench supplies both. The representation is near-perfectly speaker-identifiable — a risk inherited by any reference-based metric built on frozen SSL features, not a defect unique to one method.

Grounded in measurement

Five findings, each with a real diagnostic.

Cycle-consistency isn’t reliability

Tested against measured articulatory-inversion error on 22,854 EMA frames, a GT-free cycle-consistency reliability metric is an energy artifact: energy-controlled partial Spearman = −0.001.

Pausing, not articulation

The full metric tracks severity at ρ = 0.756; silence frames alone reproduce it (0.775). Only silence-trimming and de-leaking isolates real signal (0.644).

Early layers leak identity

A naive early-layer reference scores ρ = 0.775; under a leave-one-control-out reference it drops 0.089. Mid-network layers are leakage-robust.

Inversion is unstable on disordered input

SSL-inversion kinematics don’t track measured EMA (ρ = 0.39, n.s.); a “significant” severity correlation points the clinically wrong way.

The ground truth barely exists

Of the dysarthric TORGO speakers with articulography, only n ≈ 2 have usable sessions — a hard ceiling on validating pathological inversion with public data.

Transfer is metric-dependent

Not “frozen-SSL severity can’t transfer” — a well-designed metric transfers at scale. The honest rule: transfer must be demonstrated per metric, never assumed.
