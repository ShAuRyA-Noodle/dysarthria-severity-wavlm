\paragraph{Sample size and statistical power.}
$n = 15$ TORGO speakers (8 dysarthric) yields wide bootstrap CIs. The
dysarthric-subgroup Spearman ($n = 8$, $\rho = 0.394$, $p = 0.33$) is
non-significant at $\alpha = 0.05$ and is reported as a directional
indicator only, not as a leave-one-speaker-out cross-validation result.
We report this transparently throughout.

\paragraph{Pre-registered specificity hypothesis falsified (Track~C).}
Our pre-registration (\texttt{paper/appendix/track\_c\_preregistration.md},
locked \texttt{2026-05-08}) predicted that ATD would respond to
articulatory distortions (formant shift, partial muting) more strongly
than to acoustic distortions (pink noise, reverberation, time-stretch),
and listed the falsifying condition explicitly. That condition (locked
criterion~\#2, $\beta(\text{pink\_noise}) > \beta(\text{formant\_shift})$)
is \emph{met} at both layers: pink-noise sensitivity exceeds formant-shift
sensitivity at Layer~9 ($\rho = 0.766$ vs $0.170$) and at Layer~1
($\rho = 0.832$ vs $0.395$). ATD also does not beat MFCC on formant shift
($\rho_{\text{ATD}} - \rho_{\text{MFCC}} = -0.055$ at Layer~9). As the
pre-registration requires, we report this here as a failed hypothesis: the
perturbation study does \emph{not} establish articulatory specificity, and
we make no specificity claim from it. The one surviving positive ---
better detection of frame dropout (partial muting) than MFCC,
$\Delta\rho = +0.25$ --- speaks to ATD's use of temporal structure, not to
formant-level selectivity. The accent-specificity evidence
(\S\ref{sec:specificity}) is a separate, independently supported argument.

\paragraph{Single-corpus headline.}
The 0.756 figure is on TORGO only. Cross-corpus generalisation to
UASpeech is reported as a falsified hypothesis (the trajectory-length
analysis offers a mechanistic explanation pre-registered for follow-up);
extension to PD (PC-GITA, IPVS) and Italian dysarthria (EasyCall) is
prospective.

\paragraph{F04 leverage.}
Removing one influential speaker shifts $\rho$ from 0.756 to 0.855. We
report the leave-one-out distribution across all 15 speakers in the
supplementary.

\paragraph{Cross-corpus z-norm caveat.}
The 4-corpus pooled analysis (\S\ref{sec:specificity}) z-normalises
ATD \emph{within} each corpus before pooling. This controls for
differences in recording chain (microphone, room, sample rate) but
also collapses any absolute-scale information across corpora. The
specificity claim therefore concerns the \emph{relative position} of
each population within its corpus's ATD distribution, not the
absolute magnitude. We chose this trade-off because the alternative,
calibrating absolute ATD across corpora with different reference
constructions, is not solvable without a shared reference vocabulary.
A pooled mixed-effects model with speaker random effects is degenerate
(speaker $\to$ severity is 1:1 within corpus); we therefore report
OLS with cluster-robust standard errors on speaker, which is the
correct frequentist instrument given the dependence structure.

\paragraph{Reproducibility caveats.}
WavLM-Large is extracted in FP16 with cudnn determinism; bit-exact
reproduction across GPU drivers is not guaranteed. We release pre-
extracted embeddings on Zenodo to obviate re-extraction.

\paragraph{Ethics.}
TORGO, UASpeech, L2-ARCTIC, and VCTK are research-only releases; we do
not redistribute audio. Speaker-level ATD scores are not personally
identifying but should not be linked to clinical records without IRB.
